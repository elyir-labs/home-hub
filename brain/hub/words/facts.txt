# Fact | One short sentence that explains or adds to it. Fact at most 60, second part at most 110.
Octopuses have three hearts | Two pump blood through the gills, and the third sends it around the body.
Honey can keep for a very long time | It holds so little water that most microbes can't grow in it.
Bananas are berries, but strawberries aren't | To a botanist, a berry grows from a single flower part and holds its seeds inside.
Sound can't travel through space | Sound needs air, water or something solid to move through, and space is nearly empty.
Sunlight takes about eight minutes to reach Earth | The sunshine you see right now left the Sun a few minutes ago.
Venus spins the opposite way from most planets | Seen from Venus, the Sun would rise in the west and set in the east.
Venus takes longer to spin once than to orbit the Sun | It turns very slowly, about once every 243 Earth days.
Sea otters sometimes hold paws while they rest | It keeps them from drifting apart on the water.
Butterflies taste with their feet | Sensors on their feet tell them whether a plant is right for their eggs.
Wombats make cube-shaped droppings | The flat sides help them stay put on rocks and logs, where wombats leave them as signposts.
Hummingbirds can fly backward | The way their wings sweep lets them hover in place, too.
Lightning is hotter than the surface of the Sun | For a split second, a bolt heats the air around it to tens of thousands of degrees.
You can tell how far away lightning is | Count the seconds until the thunder; every five seconds is about a mile.
Lakes freeze from the top down | Ice floats, so it makes a lid, and fish keep swimming in the water underneath.
Earth bulges a little at the equator | Its spin pushes the middle outward, so it isn't a perfect ball.
The Moon is slowly drifting away from Earth | It moves about an inch and a half farther away every year.
We always see the same side of the Moon | It turns once for every trip around Earth, so one face always points our way.
Saturn is less dense than water | It's mostly gas, so it's lighter than the same amount of water would be.
Jupiter's Great Red Spot is a giant storm | People have been watching it swirl for well over a century.
Mars has a volcano over twice as tall as Mount Everest | It's called Olympus Mons, and it grew so big partly because the ground on Mars doesn't shift.
Mars is red because of rust | Its dust is full of iron oxide, the same stuff that makes rust red.
Sharks have been around longer than trees | Sharks swam the seas millions of years before the first forests grew.
Earth's spin is very slowly slowing down | Long ago days were shorter, and they keep growing longer by tiny amounts.
Almost all penguins live south of the equator | Galápagos penguins are the one kind that sometimes crosses into the north.
Polar bears have black skin | Their fur hairs are clear, and only look white because they scatter light.
Owls can't move their eyes | Instead they turn their heads, a long way around.
Giraffes have seven neck bones, just like people | Each of a giraffe's is just much, much longer.
Sea stars have no brain | A ring of nerves around the mouth helps their arms work together.
Honeybees dance to give directions | The waggle dance tells other bees which way to fly to find flowers.
Ants have no lungs | Air moves in and out through tiny holes along their bodies.
A rainbow is really a full circle | The ground hides the lower half; from a plane you can sometimes see the whole ring.
The sky is blue because air scatters blue light | Blue light bounces around more than red, so it reaches us from all over the sky.
Sunsets look orange because light travels farther | Near sunset, sunlight crosses more air, and most of the blue is scattered away.
Glass is made mostly from sand | Sand melted at very high heat cools into clear glass.
Pencil "lead" has no lead in it | It's graphite, a soft form of carbon, mixed with clay.
Sound travels about four times faster in water | It moves faster through water than through air, and faster still through steel.
You can see another galaxy with your own eyes | On a dark night, the Andromeda Galaxy looks like a faint, fuzzy smudge.
Stars twinkle because of Earth's air | Moving air bends their light this way and that on its way to your eyes.
Earth's inner core is solid | It's mostly iron and nickel, squeezed solid by enormous pressure.
A year on Mercury lasts 88 Earth days | It zips around the Sun faster than any other planet.
Neptune was found with math | Astronomers worked out where it should be before anyone spotted it.
Pluto is smaller than our Moon | Today it's called a dwarf planet.
A comet's tail points away from the Sun | Sunlight and the solar wind push it back, whichever way the comet travels.
Footprints on the Moon can last a very long time | With no wind or rain there, almost nothing wears them away.
You can't see the Great Wall of China from the Moon | It's very long, but far too narrow to spot from that distance.
Antarctica is a desert | A desert is a place that gets very little rain or snow, hot or cold.
The Pacific Ocean is bigger than all of Earth's land | It covers about a third of the whole planet.
Mount Everest is still growing | The land under it is still being pushed upward, a tiny bit each year.
The Amazon carries more water than any other river | More water pours from it than from the next several biggest rivers together.
The Nile flows north | It runs from the middle of Africa all the way to the Mediterranean Sea.
Snowflakes almost always have six sides | Water molecules lock together in a six-sided pattern as they freeze.
Bamboo is a kind of grass | Some kinds are among the fastest-growing plants on Earth.
Young sunflowers follow the Sun | They track it across the sky each day, then settle facing east as they bloom.
Peanuts aren't nuts | They're legumes, like peas and beans, and they grow underground.
Apples float in water | They have lots of air tucked inside, which makes them lighter than water.
Carrots weren't always orange | Early farmed carrots were often purple or yellow.
A baby kangaroo is called a joey | At birth it's about the size of a jellybean.
No two zebras have the same stripes | Each pattern is as one-of-a-kind as a fingerprint.
Tigers have striped skin, too | The stripes aren't only in the fur; they show on the skin underneath.
Wood frogs can survive being frozen | They spend winter partly frozen, then thaw out and hop off in spring.
Dolphins have whistles that work like names | Each dolphin has its own signature whistle that others recognize.
Crows can recognize human faces | They remember people for years, and can even warn other crows about them.
An ostrich's eye is bigger than its brain | Its eyes are among the largest of any animal that lives on land.
Bats aren't blind | Many see well, and some also find their way in the dark using echoes.
A rabbit's teeth never stop growing | Chewing hay and plants keeps them worn down to the right length.
Snails have thousands of tiny teeth | They sit on a ribbon-like tongue that scrapes up food.
Seahorse dads carry the babies | The male keeps the eggs in a pouch until they hatch.
Flamingos get their pink from their food | Color in the shrimp and algae they eat tints their feathers.
A group of owls is called a parliament | Many animal groups have names like this, such as a tower of giraffes.
Platypuses lay eggs | They are mammals, yet their babies hatch from eggs.
Squirrels plant trees by accident | Some of the nuts they bury and forget grow into new trees.
Beaver teeth are orange | Iron in the enamel makes them tough and gives them their color.
Elephants can use their trunks like snorkels | They can swim in deep water while breathing through the tip.
An elephant's trunk has no bones | It's made of muscle, which lets it be both strong and gentle.
Arctic terns see two summers every year | They fly from the Arctic to the Antarctic and back again each year.
Monarch butterflies travel thousands of miles | Many fly south to Mexico or California to spend the winter.
A cricket's chirps can tell you the temperature | Crickets chirp faster when it's warm and slower when it's cool.
A firefly's glow makes almost no heat | Chemicals inside it make light without wasting energy as warmth.
Some mushrooms glow in the dark | Their soft green light is sometimes called foxfire.
A blue moon isn't blue | It's a name for a second full moon in one calendar month.
The smell of rain has a name | It's called petrichor, and it comes from oils and soil that the rain stirs up.
"Alphabet" comes from two Greek letters | It joins alpha and beta, the first two letters of the Greek alphabet.
The dot over an i or a j is called a tittle | It's the small mark that sits on top of the letter.
The word "robot" comes from a Czech play | It first appeared in "R.U.R." in 1920, from a Czech word for hard work.
The sandwich is named after an earl | The Earl of Sandwich was said to like his meat tucked between slices of bread.
"Bookkeeper" has three double letters in a row | O-O, K-K, E-E, one pair right after another.
You can type "typewriter" with one row of keys | Every letter in it sits on the top row of a standard keyboard.
A pangram uses every letter of the alphabet | "The quick brown fox jumps over the lazy dog" is a famous one.
A real moth once jammed an early computer | In 1947, engineers taped it into their logbook as an actual bug.
Velcro was inspired by burrs | An engineer noticed how burrs clung to his dog's fur after a walk.
Bubble wrap was first meant to be wallpaper | When that didn't catch on, it found a better job protecting packages.
The Eiffel Tower gets taller in summer | Heat makes its iron expand, so it grows by a few inches.
Sticky notes began with a glue that barely stuck | A weak glue that seemed like a failure turned out to be just right.
The first photographs took hours to make | Early cameras needed light to shine on the plate for a very long time.
Braille was invented by a teenager | Louis Braille created his raised-dot alphabet when he was about 15.
Paper was invented in China | People there were making it about two thousand years ago.
Leap years keep the calendar in step | Earth takes about 365 and a quarter days to circle the Sun.
Earth is closest to the Sun in January | Seasons come from Earth's tilt, not its distance from the Sun.
Gallium can melt in your hand | This soft metal turns liquid just above room temperature.
Cleopatra lived nearer to today than to the Great Pyramid | The Great Pyramid was already about 2,500 years old when she was born.
Oxford University is older than the Aztec Empire | Teaching at Oxford began as early as 1096; the Aztec capital was founded in 1325.
Woolly mammoths lived while the pyramids were built | A small group survived on an Arctic island until about 4,000 years ago.
Astronauts get a little taller in space | Without gravity pressing down, their spines stretch out slightly.
