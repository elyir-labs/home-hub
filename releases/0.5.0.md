## What's new

- Plug in one of our light strips and set it up from the wall: press its button to show it is yours, then pick its room, length and colors.
- Tell a light what color to be. A colored light is drawn on the wall in the color it is.
- *What happened* shows what changed in the house while nobody was watching, and who changed it.
- Wall switches can join the hub through a bridge, and two switches on the stairs can work one light.

## Details

**Light strips.** A new strip shows up as a line in the band and a dot on the *+* door, and fills
the screen if somebody is already on *Add*. Pressing the button on the strip is what proves it is
in your house; nothing else can claim it. Once it is set up it is a light like any other, and you
can measure its length or choose its colors again later. A strip too far from the hub to hear is
set up through whichever bridge is closer.

**Color.** A bulb that can change color gets the same card as a warm one, with a color wheel on
its page. The thermostat card is now a dial that shows what the heating is doing rather than what
it is set to.

**What happened.** A page under *This house* lists what changed in the house and who changed it,
and says what a row means when you tap it. It pages back through the history rather than drawing
all of it at once.

**Bridges and wall switches.** A bridge is the small puck that lets the hub reach switches it
cannot hear on its own. Plug one into the hub with a cable and the wall asks for your Wi-Fi once,
then asks where the bridge will live. From there it can let a new switch into the house, and a
switch can be named by pressing it. Two switches on one light, such as the top and bottom of a
stair, now work that light together. A bridge can also glow softly as you arrive at night and
dim again on its own. Each bridge has a page saying whether it is fine, and the wall tells you
which ones need an update.

**Everything the house has.** *Add* has one list of everything in the house, which scrolls, and
anything you are done with can be removed and stays removed. The wall no longer shows things the
household never bought.

**Share this house.** The house can be added to Apple Home, Google Home or Alexa, and the page
says what will and will not be handed over before anybody agrees.

**Fixes.** Setting the hub's code during setup can no longer be skipped. Without a code, anyone
on the Wi-Fi could ask the hub for its backup, and a hub that still has no code now refuses.
An update that took longer than a few minutes now says what it is doing instead of repeating "a
few minutes".
