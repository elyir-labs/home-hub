## What's new

- The hub installs its own updates overnight now, and puts the old one back by itself if a new one doesn't start.
- What changed appears on the wall the next morning, in plain words, and stays under *This hub* afterwards.
- A phone that has the house on its home screen stops showing an old version of itself after the hub updates.
- The hub's inner workings no longer answer to anything on the Wi‑Fi without a sign-in; the house's own screens still do.
- The hub restarts itself if it ever stops responding, and its own record-keeping can no longer fill its disk.

## Details

The hub will only install an update it can check came from us: every release is signed, and a hub
that cannot check one leaves itself alone and says so rather than installing it anyway. It also
takes updates a few houses at a time rather than all at once, so a bad one can be stopped before it
reaches everybody.

Updating happens in the small hours, at a minute of the hub's own choosing, and never while somebody
is still asking the house for things. One switch under *This hub* turns it off and it stays off.

The parts the hub runs for itself that had no sign-in of their own used to be reachable by anything
on the same Wi‑Fi, with nothing asked of it. They now answer only on the hub, and the one that carries
messages between them has a password of its own that is made fresh on each hub. The two parts that
already had a sign-in still answer on the Wi‑Fi behind it, as before.
