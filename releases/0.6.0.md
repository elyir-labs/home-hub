## What's new

- Your phones can use the house when you are away from home. Setup offers it once at the end, with the price said plainly, and it is optional.
- A second wall screen joins the house you already have, asks which room it is in, and opens there.
- Thermostats, car chargers, home batteries and 3D printers each get their own card, and the wall says when the power goes out and when it is back.
- A screen that cannot reach the hub says so, instead of blaming a light.

## Details

**Using the house away from home.** The last step of setup asks whether the house wants its own web
address. The house is complete without it. Saying yes gives the house a name the household
chooses, and paying happens on a phone, never on the wall screen. Each phone on People is set to
*Home only* or *Anywhere*, and says when the house last saw it, so two phones with the same name
can be told apart. A phone set to *Anywhere* switches to the web address once, and still reaches
the hub directly while it is at home. A house that said *Not now* is asked again the first time a
phone is set to *Anywhere*.

**One app for every house.** On a phone, every house it has joined lives in one app, and moving
a phone to the web address happens inside it.

**A second wall screen.** A wall screen started in a house that already has a hub says whose house
it found, asks for the passcode or a yes from another screen, then asks which room it is in. It
opens on that room and wakes to it. Renaming the screen, or the hub moving to a new address, does
not lose it.

**Typing on the wall.** The wall screen brings its own keyboard: a keypad for codes, letters for
names and towns, and for the box at the top of Home, the house's own suggestions above the
letters. Phones and tablets keep theirs.

**When a screen cannot reach the hub.** A screen checks in with the hub every half minute. If it
cannot, a line along the bottom says changes are not reaching the hub, since when, and that the
switches on the wall still work. A card you tap while cut off says *Still on* instead of
pretending. A screen also keeps finding the hub when the Wi-Fi stops answering to hub.local.

**Thermostats.** A thermostat is drawn as its own ring, with the setting marked. You can choose
which room it keeps comfortable, and when it sets itself to Away or Sleep it says so in words.

**Car chargers.** A charger is a card in its room, and Home says when the car is charging.

**Power and the sun.** With a home battery, the wall says when the power goes out and how long the
battery will last, when it is back, and when there is sun to spare. The sun in the weather picture
warms with what the roof is making, and a page shows where the power is going.

**What stays on Home.** From its own page, a thing can be kept on Home even when it is off (up to
four on each screen), or taken off Home altogether.

**Adding things.** Something just added shows up without a refresh. When an add brings several
things, the end of it asks each one's room right there. A radio the hub already uses is no longer
listed as found nearby.

**3D printers.** The house finds the printers on its Wi-Fi. You add one at the printer itself,
then choose its room, and you are only asked once. A print in progress leads *Your afternoon*,
and each printer has its own page. A printer that moves to a new address is found there.

**Light strips and the roofline.** A strip that is staying off says why on its tile, and a tap
explains it rather than offering a switch that cannot work. Adding a second strip asks one
question, and two strips can be joined into one light or split apart later. The roofline is one
light however many controllers it has. It keeps its own evenings, an occasion decides how it
looks, and *Hold it still* stops the motion. A roofline's page draws the roof, so a dark end is
easy to spot. A strip can also be removed from its own page.

**What the lights tell you.** A page under *This house* lists the signals the lights can give,
such as arriving, leaving or a door left open, each with a switch and a *Try*. A light strip can
show a signal along its length and go back to how it was afterwards.

**Bridges and strips take their own fixes.** Bridges and strips set up from now on get updates
from the hub, and go back to what they had if one does not work. A strip no longer freezes after
setup, and comes back as it was left.

**Clearer words.** The words on the screens were rewritten for somebody new to the house. A thing is *added*
and *removed*, settings have one name each, a restart says how deep it goes, and *What happened*
tells its story in plain words.

**Light or dark.** Each screen can be light or dark, chosen on the Look page for that screen only.

**Starting up.** The wall starts by writing *elyir* by hand. The dot of the i lights like a lamp,
moves into the house, and breathes there until the hub is ready.

**The wall screen.** What is playing sits on glass, and its volume is a line you can drag. The
resting screen was redrawn, with the weather in its corner.

**Fixes.** The hub restarts a part of itself left stopped after a power cut, and *What happened*
says when that happens. *Needs a look* speaks up if it keeps happening. A camera's live view is
never open to anyone outside the house. Sharing the house with Apple Home, Google Home and Alexa
works again, and a tap on it answers the first time. hub.local came back after an update that
had lost it. A release that was checked and approved now installs instead of stopping halfway.
