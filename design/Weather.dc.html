One cloud, every condition, both lights

Not thirty drawings — one illustration built from parts, driven by the same {clouds, rain, snow, fog, lightning, wind} that wxOf() already hands the sky. Night is not one of those parts, and it is not a condition: the house reports the weather, the sun’s elevation says which light it is in. So every condition has to hold up twice. Day on the left of each pair, night on the right. Only the clear sky carries two names.

Every mark below is illustration()’s, and every sky is palette()’s, read out of src/sky.ts at 40° and -20° of sun — not drawn again here. What changes between a pair: the sky, grayed by cloud, darkened by rain, flattened by fog, in either light; the disc, a gold sun and a bitten silver moon crossfading in one place; the stars a thin enough sky lets through; and the cloud body, lit white by day, only moonlit by night. Nothing else moves. The bolt is the same color in both, because lightning brings its own light.
