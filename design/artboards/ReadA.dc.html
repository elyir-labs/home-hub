Today

The strip is dark, and the wall says it is on

Wall · 1440 × 900Open aloneCopy linkBeside the panel

Drawn from the shipped panel

The mock house’s kitchen, card for card where the shipped panel puts them at 1440 × 900, at dusk. The Under-cabinet strip reads On, 100%, warm, exactly as it would if it were lit.

It is not lit. Its controller was moved to a 24 V supply, the guard held the run off at boot, and it has said why every ten seconds since. The hub files a leaf it does not know and does nothing with it. That is exactly the problem: the tile is the hub’s memory of what it asked for, and nothing tells it otherwise.

Held dark, and why · direction A of three

In the room: the light says it where you reach for it

Chosen 1 Oct
Wall · 1440 × 900Open aloneCopy linkBeside the panel

The tile stops claiming a state it cannot have. It reads Staying off, with the reason in a few words under it and a small shield where the bulb was, in the lamp color: the strip is being looked after rather than broken.

The tap. A light that cannot come on cannot honor a tap, so on this one tile a tap opens why instead of doing nothing. Holding opens the same pane, as on every light.

For — the fault is in the room and so is the fix. No new surface, no new sheet, no new rule about when to interrupt. And it is always true.
Against — it is quiet to the point of hiding. A roofline on a timer is in no room anybody opens, and the first anybody learns of it is a dark house at dusk.

It is a job, so it goes on Needs a look

Its row is kept

Needs a look is already the house’s list of what it cannot fix by itself, a row each, every row carrying its own acts. A held run is exactly that: something only hands can put right…
