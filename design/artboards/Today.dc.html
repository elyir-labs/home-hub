1Nothing to type into. What you remember is a board’s title or a phrase from a note (“held dark”, “the band”), and 467 titles and 481 notes can only be found by opening collections until one looks right.
2Folder order, three to a screen. The page is thirteen rows deep, newest work nowhere in particular. Thirteen cards are titled with the folder name: forget, healed, rest, nightfall.
3A card shows how many, never what happened. 9 boards, 8 notes — not whether it was decided, which direction won, when, or whether it is built.
4Six page names wrap the bar onto two lines, and “Not chosen” is a tab like any other. On the canvas the board that won looks exactly like the ones that lost.
5It opens at fit — 17% here. Every note is too small to read, and they are the reason the collection exists. Getting to a sentence is a zoom and a pan, every time.
6Read is the right idea, drawn as one long column. Notes and boards in order, but nothing to jump to: no sections for Today, A, B and C, no outline, nothing to say which one won. The notes’ headings are capitals because that is how they are typed into canvas.json.
7Dark only, everywhere. The colors are written into the page, so there is no light mode to switch to, even for an afternoon of reading notes.
Every one of these is information canvas.json already has, unused. Titles, notes, page names, the reading order and the launch board are all read on every request; the dates are in git. None of it reaches the front page except a count.
