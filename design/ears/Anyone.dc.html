B — everything you already own is an ear

Not one courier. Every thing of ours that has power and a radio, and the hub asks whoever heard it best.

The same errand as A, without the single point of it. A puck, a plug, a strip already on the wall — each one is listening anyway, each one reports the strength it heard a knock at, and the hub hands the job to the loudest. The hub’s own radio stops being special and becomes one ear among several, which is what it should have been from the start.

The wall shows none of this. It is a ranked list inside the hub and the household is told only that a light strip is here.

For

It gets better the longer somebody has owned our things, and it never needs a particular one. It also kills the special case: the hub’s radio is just an ear that is usually in the wrong room, so a mini PC with no Bluetooth is not a broken hub — it is a house with one fewer ear. And the loudest ear is a measurement, so this is the first answer that could say which room the trouble is in.

Against

It puts a GATT central and an errand loop in the smallest thing we ship — in a plug, in a strip whose one job is to be a light and not to stutter while it runs somebody else’s handshake. It is A’s risk, taken four times over. And it does not solve the first purchase: a house with one of our things in it has no ear at all, and that is the house most likely to be setting something up.
