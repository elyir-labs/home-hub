A / The stone: four things to press, and the rest said aloud

A smooth pebble that sits on the coffee table and fills a palm. A ring that turns, a pad in its middle that tilts to move and presses for OK, Home below, and a key on top to talk. Scenes and anything else are said, not pressed. It is the least like a remote, and the least to learn.

Scenes are said. The same sentences the house already understands when typed. With the TV off, the light blinks once for done.

For it
  • Four things to press, all findable without looking. A guest can use it in a second.
  • It is an object for the coffee table, not a gadget. Nobody has one like it.
Against it
  • Scenes need a voice. A quiet house, or someone who would rather not talk to it, gets them only on the TV.
  • No Back or Play key, which a streaming box's own screens lean on. Home and a held OK stand in for them.
  • A pebble does not point.
To find out
  • Whether a 46 mm ring is comfortable under one thumb while the stone is held.
  • Whether Home doing two jobs is learned in a day or keeps catching people out.
Drawn at 3.4 px to the millimeter. Charges on a USB-C cable or a small dish. Every direction knows which room it is in, wakes when it is picked up, and can be found by asking.
