C / The dial: it lives on the table, and you turn it

A heavy round dial that stays where it is put, on the dish that charges it. The whole top turns and presses. Turning moves along the room's things, and a press picks one so turning sets its level. With the TV off, turning dims the room. It is never lost down the sofa.

Turning walks along the row. A press picks the lit one and turning then sets it. A streaming box's grid of apps is walked one step at a time.

On the side table

It stays where it lives, charging on its dish with a faint glow under it. It works as a bedroom's own control, with no TV at all.

For it
  • Turning is the most natural way to say how much. Dimming feels like a real dimmer.
  • Never lost, never flat: it lives on its dish. A second one is a room's control by the bed.
Against it
  • No arrows. Walking a streaming box's grid of apps by turning is slow, so for watching it is the weakest.
  • Turning means two things, moving or setting, and a press switches between them. That has to be learned.
  • It does not point, and it is not for a lap.
To find out
  • How heavy it must be not to slide when turned with one finger.
  • Whether turning reads as left and right on an Apple TV well enough to use.
