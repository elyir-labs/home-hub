Shorter, longer. One light at a time, on the thing itself, with no number to read.

The row opens in place rather than opening a sheet, the strip comes on to the length it currently believes, and two buttons move its far end. Hold either one and the end walks. Nothing is timed and nothing is measured — you are looking at the strip, and you stop when the light stops where the strip stops.

What a hold does

A tap is one light. Held, the end walks — slowly at first so a single light is still reachable, then faster, so being thirty out is a second rather than thirty taps. The strip is the progress bar; nothing on the screen has to be.

For

It is the product’s own move, applied to the one thing that goes stale. You identify a thing by doing something to it and watching what happens — the same as pressing a switch to say which room it is in. No number on the wall, which is what direction B had to give up. And it cannot be missed: nothing is running, so you can look away and come back.

Against

At sixty lights to the metre you may not be able to see which light is last when the whole strip is lit — a warm strip behind a television is a glow, not a row of dots. That is the argument for C. And a big correction is a long hold, where a drag would be one movement.

Saying so is what keeps the length beat one tap. Somebody who does not know it can be fixed will sit through the fill again to get it exact, which is the beat’s one known weakness being paid for twice. The line is only honest because this card exists, so it ships with it and not before.

Both ways out are on the card. That’s it keeps what you moved to; Measure it again is the fill, unchanged, for somebody who did not cut three lights off but added two metres. The fine path does not replace the coarse one.
