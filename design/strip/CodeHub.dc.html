What the other three had to give up
A On the box. Honest and finished, and it gives up the thing Knock was proudest of: eleven digits before the wall will do anything.
B The hub works it out. One algorithm loose and every unit we have ever sold is open to whoever holds it.
C A phone reads it off the thing. Cannot stand alone — something still has to be printed for it to read.

All three answer the same hard question — how a code gets in from outside the radio. D is the only one that never has to answer it.

Nobody printed this anywhere.

The strip has no passcode to give, and that is the point. A production device stores only the SPAKE2+ verifier — on ESP32 GetSetupPasscode returns NOT_IMPLEMENTED, and Espressif’s own guidance is that the verifier is installed “and not the actual passcode”. Asked over the link that carried the Wi‑Fi and the broker in Ours, it mints one instead: a passcode for this window only, its verifier derived on the chip. The wall shows a code that did not exist a second ago and will not exist in fifteen minutes.

For

B fails because it derives. This does not even store. A hub that can work out any unit’s code from its chip id makes every strip we ever sell commissionable by anybody holding the algorithm. Here there is no algorithm, no list kept at the factory, and no passcode anywhere at all until somebody in the room asks for one: the chip’s own DRBG makes it, the verifier comes off it, and both are gone when the window shuts. And where C needed a phone held against the object, this needs only the wall the household already bought.

Against

It does not replace A, and saying it does would be the mistake. A strip in a house with no hub of ours needs a number from outside the radio, and that is the label. This is what A becomes once our hub is there: a second act, not a fourth candidate. Two things the screen must not hide — while this window is open the label’s own code does not work, the fresh verifier having taken its place; and an enhanced window never advertises over Bluetooth, so the phone has to be on the house Wi‑Fi. Fifteen minutes is the specification’s ceiling, not our choice.
