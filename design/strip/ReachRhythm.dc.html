Demoted, not deleted.

This is the screen that shipped on 21 September as what everybody got, and it was right about the hard part and wrong about who it was for. Counting four groups of flashes is a real chore, and a proof that needs two metres of light is no proof at all on a sensor. So it stops being the door and becomes the rung below it: reached only by tapping It has no button I can reach, on a strip somebody has already mounted.

Why it, rather than the rung the ladder names

The door canvas puts ShowMe here — the thing lights up and you say yes. For a strip that would mean the gate moving off the strip and onto the hub, so anything that can speak first gets in, which is the link the first firmware was condemned for. The rhythm keeps the gate on the strip and keeps a real secret behind SRP6a. It is built, it is proven on silicon, and it costs nothing to keep.

What it still costs

Everything the complaint said: four numbers, read from across a room, off a thing that may be behind a television — which is exactly where a strip with no reachable button is. I can’t see it either stays a real button, because from here there is nothing below it and the honest answer is to say so.

The one thing that is new in the firmware

The rhythm is minted when it is asked for, not at every boot. A verifier cannot be swapped inside a session, so asking for it tears the door down and reopens it with a verifier made from four fresh digits — which means the strip is quiet until somebody asks, and a stranger who forces the question for the fun of it has only made the strip harder to take: they still cannot see the flashes.
