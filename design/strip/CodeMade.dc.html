No number, because the hub works the passcode out. We make these, so we can decide at manufacture what a given unit’s code is — derived from its serial and a secret the hub also has — and the wall then asks the one question design/puck/Knock.dc.html says it should: is the thing that just lit up yours.

For

One tap, and the promise of the whole product holds: plug it in, the wall notices, say yes. It is the only direction here where our hub is better at this than anybody else’s app, which is a reason to own one. The label still carries a code for other ecosystems, so nothing is lost.

Against, and it is serious

Every unit we sell becomes commissionable by anybody holding the derivation. The serial is in the advertisement. So the secret is the only thing between a stranger in range and somebody’s light — and it is in every hub we ship, which means it leaks the first time anybody reads a flash chip. A passcode that can be computed is not a passcode.

It is also the one option that fails certification: the standard requires the passcode to be unguessable and forbids a fixed relationship to anything public.
