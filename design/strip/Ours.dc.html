The broker is the thing item 2a has been an empty string about since Matter came in. Here it is not a problem to solve later: it rides the session that is already open, at the one moment we are standing in the room. And the third chip is what makes CodeHub possible.

The identity check is two meters of light.

This is the beat design/puck/Knock.dc.html argued for and Matter took away. The standard will not let a commissioner in without a passcode it got from outside the radio, so our own door is the only place that claim can survive — and on a strip, of all things, the proof is overwhelming. A bridge puck had one LED to identify itself with.

For

Everything drawn in this collection works, on the day the strip is plugged in, with no Vendor ID, no attestation certificate and no lab. The color question gets its real bytes and the fill gets its number back, because the channel carries both. And it is one beat shorter than the other lane, not longer: the household never meets a code at all.

Against

This half is ours to get wrong, and we already did once. The first firmware put a household’s Wi‑Fi password on an unauthenticated BLE link, and deleting that was the whole reason Matter came in. Bringing our own door back brings the responsibility back with it: protocomm SECURITY_2, not the SECURITY_1 that was on air, and a second service in an image whose free heap nobody has measured since it left Arduino. Matter’s PASE is audited by people who are not us. Ours is not.

Why this is not a convenience

Everything on the bench today runs on test vendor id 0xFFF1. Apple Home and Google Home both took one on 20 September, so the gate is not the commissioner — it is that a strip on a test id cannot be sold: it names itself TEST_VENDOR, it is in no compliance ledger, it may not wear the logo, and it carries no certificate of its own. Membership, a certificate in every unit at manufacture, testing per product at a lab. This door is how a strip reaches a household before any of that, and gains the other lane by an update afterwards.
