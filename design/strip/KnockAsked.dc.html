And what the household sees

A strip in a spare room says nothing. A strip somebody wants says so within fifteen minutes of being asked, every time, identically. The cost is that the asking is a press on the object itself.

For

The tightest of the three, and the only one whose promise fits in a sentence a household will believe: it is findable when you ask. Every attempt after the first is identical to the first, which neither of the others manages.

Against

A household who does not know the button exists is exactly where B leaves them, only fifteen minutes sooner. This board first said something stronger and it was invented: that it asks somebody to reach a thing they have just taped behind a television. Nothing established that order. A strip is unboxed, plugged in, set up, and placed — in that order, in your hands, like everything else in this house — and where the button sits on a real unit is a hardware decision nobody has taken yet (item 9).

What C costs to build, and what it is not

A short press on the button that already exists for the factory‑reset hold, which is free in hardware. What it costs afterwards depends on where the button ends up, and that is still open. This is not the case the router‑reboot rule covers — that one is a strip somebody has already set up announcing itself again, unasked. This is a strip nobody has ever taken, with nothing yet to protect. The two look similar on a radio and are opposite in a house.
