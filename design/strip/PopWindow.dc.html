There is nothing to type. The door is open for three minutes after it is plugged in, once.

Exactly what Ours drew, and what it costs.

SEC1: an x25519 handshake with no proof of possession. The session is encrypted, so nothing in radio range can read the Wi‑Fi password. What it cannot do is tell our hub from anything else that speaks first. So the door is gated instead of locked: open for three minutes after power is applied, closed forever by the first session that completes, and the light says which strip is asking.

For

The sheet on Ours survives untouched, and it is one beat shorter than every alternative. Passive listening gets nothing. Three minutes after a plug-in, once, in somebody’s own home, is a narrow window; and the first completed session shutting the door means a second party cannot follow the first.

Against

An active attacker in radio range during those three minutes is the hub. That is the class of risk this project already paid once to remove — the first firmware’s sin was an unauthenticated link, and this is an encrypted but unauthenticated one. Theirs already ruled that being nearby is not proof; on BLE it is proximity, which is weaker than a secret, not a substitute for one. Choosing C is choosing to say that out loud on the box.
