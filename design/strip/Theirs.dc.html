Strip

They typed the code off the label into whatever app they already use, and got a color light. Nothing of ours was in the house, or it was and nobody thought to start there. Either way it is done, it works, and it is theirs.

There’s a strip of ours in the house.

It’s set up in another app and it stays there. We can ask it two things that app has no way to ask — which order its colors come out in, and how far along the wall it goes.

It’s lit now, so you can check it is the one you mean
Set it up here too Leave it

Nothing changes in the other app either way.

And if they leave it

Nothing happens and nothing asks again — an offer that returns every week is a house nagging its owner about a light that already works. What stays is one quiet row on the strip’s pane saying where it was set up, and that row is the way back.

The door back is on the Wi‑Fi, not on the radio.

This is the part that was not obvious. Bluetooth’s only job was getting the strip onto a network, and somebody else already did that. What is missing is the handshake that hands over a broker — so the strip offers that over mDNS, as an ordinary service on the LAN, and our hub finds it the way it finds anything else. Two transports for one handshake: Bluetooth before there is a network, mDNS after. Their fabric is never touched, nothing is re‑commissioned, and the strip does not leave their app.

For

A household that bought a strip before they bought a hub is not punished for the order they did it in, and they do not have to factory‑reset anything to be rewarded for it. It also quietly repairs Foreign.dc.html, which drew the pane for a strip we can never ask: with this, most of those are strips we have simply not asked yet.

Against, and it is the whole of the design

On Bluetooth, being in the room was itself a kind of proof. On the Wi‑Fi there is no proximity at all, so anything on the LAN could ask a strip to be its own — and docs/away.md has already said that being on the Wi‑Fi must get you nothing. So the light in that sheet is not a friendly touch, it is the gate: the strip lights, a person confirms, and the window is short. If that tap is ever made optional this board becomes a hole.

How the house knows it is one of ours to offer at all

Not from the vendor and model: the first commissioning reported TEST_VENDOR and TEST_PRODUCT, which is what every device built on a test id says. Until a real Vendor ID exists it is our own mDNS service that answers this, which is the same thing that carries the handshake — so the offer appears only for a strip that can actually take it.
