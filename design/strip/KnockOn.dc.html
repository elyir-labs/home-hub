And what the household sees

Nothing, ever, that needs explaining. The wall finds the strip whenever somebody gets round to it, and the second attempt looks exactly like the first. A household cannot get this state wrong because there is only one of it.

For

The only version with no cliff in it. Plug a strip in, get called away, come back on Saturday — it is still there. No sentence has to be written on a box, in a manual or on the wall, which is the strongest argument any of these three has.

Against

A strip nobody ever sets up advertises for the rest of its life. A radio talking in somebody's house for no reason, and a thing any passer‑by can see is unclaimed. It also cuts against the instinct behind the router‑reboot rule: this house does not leave doors open merely because nobody shut them.

What A costs to build, and what it is not

One mechanism of ours: reopen the window whenever CHIP lets it lapse, for the life of the strip. This is not the case the router‑reboot rule covers — that one is a strip somebody has already set up announcing itself again, unasked, several times a year. This is a strip nobody has ever taken, still in the state it left the factory in, with nothing yet to protect. The two look similar on a radio and are opposite in a house.
