B — two days, then dark

Long enough to be a weekend. Short enough to end.

CHIP looks as though it has the knob — ENABLE_BLE_EXT_ANNOUNCEMENT raises the window cap from 900 seconds to 48 hours for a device nobody has commissioned. It does not compile. It is default n, nobody builds that path, and CHIP's own BLEManagerImpl drops a nodiscard error under ‑Werror. So the window is reopened from our code instead, until the two days are up.

And what the household sees

For two days, nothing that needs explaining. After that the strip is silent and looks broken, and the household has to know that something wakes it — which means a sentence somewhere, and the light going out rather than simply stopping.

For

It covers the story that actually happens: plugged it in, got called away, came back tomorrow. Forty‑eight hours is the number Matter's own authors chose for this exact case, so the behavior is theirs even though the mechanism has to be ours. And it ends, so a strip nobody wanted goes quiet by itself rather than talking for years.

Against

It still has a cliff, and an unannounced cliff is the worst kind. Two days later the thing is dead to every scanner and looks faulty. Taking B means owing a sentence the household will read long before they need it, which is the sort of sentence nobody reads.

What B costs to build, and what it is not

Not the one line this board first claimed. CHIP's own extended announcement does not build in this connectedhomeip, and patching it would put the fix outside this repository where nobody else could reproduce it. So B costs what A costs — reopening the window ourselves — with a budget on it. That is a worse answer than the board was sold on, and still the right one. This is not the case the router‑reboot rule covers — that one is a strip somebody has already set up announcing itself again, unasked. This is a strip nobody has ever taken, with nothing yet to protect. The two look similar on a radio and are opposite in a house.
