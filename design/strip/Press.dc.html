What the strip does, and when.

The wall is drawn from the board on the door canvas. This is the half that is firmware, and it has never been drawn: the gate is on the strip, not on the hub. A session can be opened by anything in radio range and get exactly nowhere — the credentials are refused until somebody in the room has touched the object.

KnockingLit end to end from the moment it has power, and steady. Not a rhythm: a steady light is a thing you can point at, and it says nothing a stranger could use.
PressedOne bright blink, on the strip itself, so the press is answered by the thing that was pressed. The gate is open for two minutes, once.
Letting inThe Wi‑Fi and where we are arrive together, in the session that was already open. It holds the steady glow until the household’s own light has something to say.
Nobody cameTwo days, then the same glow drained rather than dark — unchanged. Going dark is what a broken strip does.
The same button, two lengths

A short press is counted on the way up, before the hold arms at one second. Holding for five is still “forget the house”, and it still turns the strip red at one second to say so — so the two cannot be confused by anybody doing either of them on purpose. And the press is only ever counted once the button has been seen let go, which is the rule that was already there: GPIO 0 is held down to flash and sits low on some boards, and without it a strip would forget the house five seconds into every boot.
