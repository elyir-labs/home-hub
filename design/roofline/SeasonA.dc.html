For

It is how the household thinks about it: Halloween is a stretch of evenings, not a button. Set once, it comes back every October with nobody touching anything — the SP107E’s whole job, done by the house.

The rule’s reason survives in a narrower rule: an occasion may light only what it is allowed to, outside, inside its own hours, and the band says so two days ahead and again as it happens.

Against

The rule gets an exception, and exceptions are where trust leaks: “nothing turns on” becomes “nothing turns on, except…”, and the list of what is allowed lives inside every occasion.

And the roof’s hours belong to the roof, not to Halloween. A household that wants it on every evening, holiday or not, has to invent an occasion called “Evenings”.
