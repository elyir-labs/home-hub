What the house knows now

Three strips, adopted one at a time, each with its own pane, its own Off and its own name. A box with two runs shows the same light on both until it is told otherwise (docs/strip.md, item 51).

Christmas on this roof is three trips to three panes, and the household calls all of it the roofline.

Why a chase cannot cross a box

Each box draws its own frames on its own clock, from its own first light. Nothing tells the middle box where the left one ended, or that its first run goes up the gable toward the other, not away.

Set all three chasing and the roof shows it: two runs crash at the peak, two at the garage, and none of the three is in step.

Where this comes from

The hardware’s choice, C, one per run: a small board where each run starts, so there is no long data wire from the garage to an eave. It moved the problem into software on purpose — boards that keep one clock, and a brain that adopts several things as one light. These boards are that software’s first question.
