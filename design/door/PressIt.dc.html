Holding the thing is the proof.

Everything we make has a button already, because everything needs a way to be forgotten. A short press on it is the whole handshake: the household does nothing but touch the object they just unpacked, and the hub knows that whoever answered is in the room with it.

For

The strongest proof on this canvas and the least work to use. Possession, not a guess: no keyspace to argue about, nothing on a label to leak, nothing derived from a chip. It needs no light, so it is the same gesture on a sensor as on two metres of strip, and it is what every product a household has ever set up already does.

Against

It assumes a button that can be reached at the moment of setup. That is true when a thing is unboxed, plugged in and set up before it is placed — which is the ordinary order and the one this canvas had wrong until 21 September — and it stops being true for anything already mounted, wired in, or sealed. It also makes a claim on every board we lay out, and that claim has never been written down: a button, reachable, on the outside.
