It has to be near a switch. The outlet is only where the power is.

brilliant.md already settles why the bridge is its own object and not a radio in the hub: it has to sit within about −80 dBm of any one switch, and that has to hold whether the hub is a Pi, a NUC or a VM. So the spec is proximity to a switch — the socket is a constraint it is forced to satisfy, not the thing it is for. Nothing here is chosen.

Which part is reversible

The connector can change in a second revision. The shell, the diffusing face and where the antenna sits cannot. The plug in the shell is the most finished of these four and the least reversible — which is the argument for it being the second decision, taken after a few have been placed by people who did not build them.

All four keep the hub’s port

Every shape keeps the seat that matters: the hub writes Wi‑Fi, broker and keys down the CDC console the puck already speaks, so the in‑box bridge never asks anybody for a password. A 100 mm cable in the box serves that as well as a molded plug — and keeps the radio out of a metal chassis, the worst RF position in the house.

The thing no drawing shows

Whether the walk survives a cold boot. Only the dock stays alive in your hand; in the other three you unplug it, it dies, and each candidate socket has to prove itself from scratch — a 6 s scan, three empty passes before red. Nobody stands in a hallway through that twice. Shorten it, or carry the charge.
