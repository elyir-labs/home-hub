The instrument is the bridge itself.

Both halves of setting one up end the same way: you unplug it, walk off with it in your hand, and find it a socket. The wall is on the wall and the phone is in your other hand — so the thing that has to answer “is here good?” is the object. One light, three things it can say. Nobody has to be told what they mean; you stand there until it goes green.

Why not on the wall

The wall panel is the one screen in the house that cannot follow you. Every other step of setting a bridge up belongs there — it is where the house talks — but this step is a walk down a corridor holding the thing. Put it on the wall and you have designed a screen nobody is standing in front of.

What it costs

Three light states have to survive the Wi‑Fi being down, the broker being down, and the mesh being out of range — which is exactly the point: the light is the only thing that still works when all three are. It is also the one part of this design that is firmware, not panel, so it should be settled before anything is built.
