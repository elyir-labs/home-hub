Three small things that were saying the wrong word.

None of these is a layout problem. Each is a control whose appearance meant something other than what it did.

one · which rooms do you have?
A room you already have is not a dimmed copy of one you just picked.
before — two states, and they were the same state
Kitchen already in the house — opacity .7 · Porch you tapped it a second ago
The first read as unavailable. Glance away and you could no longer tell which rooms you had chosen from the ones that were always there.
after — three states, three shapes
Kitchen Hallway Porch
Outlined — already yours, nothing to do. Filled dark — tap to add. Filled light — you just picked it. Captioned once under the lede: “The outlined ones are already in the house.”
two · where is home?
The loudest button on the screen was the one that walks past the question.
before
Keep it …and nothing else, until you had already chosen a place
The real action lived inside LocationPicker and only appeared once a town was picked. So the screen’s whole visible offer was skip, and a person who did want to set a location had to find a second action row further down.
after — there is always a primary, and it is never the way past
Continue a place is set — the hub found one, or you picked one
Skip for now nothing is set — and it says so plainly
three · another room…
A magnifying glass on a field that adds.
Another room… → Another room…
There is nothing to search. Typing here makes a room that did not exist a moment ago.
