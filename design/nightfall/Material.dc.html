The material

Nightfall glass

One pane, six layers, always in this order. The blur is what the pane is made of; the rim is its edge catching the light; the sweep is that light traveling across it; the inner shade is its thickness; the drop is the air underneath. Take any one away and the rest stops reading as glass.

Left of each pair is the sky’s own ramp at that elevation; right of it is the veiled middle band — the surface a card actually sits on, and the only thing anything else here is measured against. It travels from L .13 to L .49 across the day, which is why nothing in this system is allowed a fixed color. The four blooms Nightfall is built on are laid ON that at low alpha: they are character, not lightness.

A card that already means something with its color does not get overpainted: a cover and a camera frame bring their own. The other two are never given an absolute color — they are given a distance from the field, always this much lighter and always this far round the hue wheel. Fixed fills look right at one hour and wrong at another, because the sky moves further across a day than the gap between any two of these swatches.
