How it moves

Nightfall glass

This screen hangs on a wall and is looked at from across a room, so nothing here is quick and nothing here is a bounce. Weight is the whole idea: a pane of glass leaves fast and arrives slowly, and it settles rather than stopping.

The settle
cubic-bezier(.32,.72,0,1)

Everything a finger starts. Leaves at once, arrives without a stop.

The pane arriving
cubic-bezier(.12,.78,.24,1)

Half the distance in the first 100ms, home at 400. Expo would be over by 150 and read as a cut.

The pane leaving
cubic-bezier(.4,0,.6,1)

Even at both ends, and short. Leaving should never take as long as arriving.

The house's own
cubic-bezier(.2,.7,.2,1)

Already in panel.css as --ease: the rail, and anything the house decides by itself.

Above the scale sit two things that never end: the field, drifting 3% over 46s, and the orb, whose two blooms turn against each other at 14s and 19s. Neither is a transition — they are what stops a wall panel looking switched off.

Eight moves, and the panel has no ninth
1 · The wake
680ms settle · 64ms apart · y+26, blur 14→0

The field blooms first, then the cards rise into it left to right. The blur coming off is what makes them land as glass rather than fade in as boxes.

2 · The press
scale .972 · 120ms down, 260ms back

Down fast, up slow: a finger stops a pane instantly and lets it go gradually. Never a bounce — glass does not overshoot.

3 · The pane
400ms up · 300ms down · object 700ms · bar leaves 60ms early

A device does not grow out of its tile. A pane rises from the bottom edge and stops short of the top bar, so the row you came from is still there, dimmed, above it. The object inside travels further and keeps settling after the pane has landed — which is what puts it INSIDE the glass rather than printed on it.

4 · The swipe
650ms travel · past: blur 5, opacity .6, and 150 of the 460 given back · velocity blur 2.5px, 150ms in 500ms off

The rail has a focal plane, and depth is one idea rather than three: further back is blurrier, dimmer AND slower. The card you have just passed gives back 150 of the rail's 460, so the one in focus catches it and slides over the top — and the sky, which gives back nearly all of it, is only the far end of the same ramp. The readout hung in front of the sky fades out, because a temperature you have swiped away from is not what you are reading any more.

Your evening Rooms
5 · Changing tab
420ms · cross-dissolve ±14px, in the direction of travel

The bar itself does not move — no sliding pill, no underline. Only the word's weight changes and the content underneath gives way.

6 · Listening
scale 1.18 · 320ms in, 520ms out · one ring, once

Lit from inside, not a gradient painted on. Two blooms turning against each other at 14s and 19s, behind their own frost.

Try “kitchen lights off”
7 · The command box
460ms of width · the hint fades in 180ms behind it

The same orb, opened. Width leads and the words follow, so the box is already the right size by the time there is anything to read in it.

8 · The weather turning
1200ms linear · rain falls on 1.6s, three ranks

The drawing and the field behind it read the same numbers, so they cannot disagree about the hour. Drops stay on the glass only while it is actually raining.

Under prefers-reduced-motion, all eight collapse to opacity

Nothing travels, nothing scales, nothing blurs, and the field stops drifting. The wake becomes a 180ms fade, the pane becomes a cut, the rail jumps. Every duration above still applies — only the property being animated changes, which is why none of the timings need a second set of numbers.
